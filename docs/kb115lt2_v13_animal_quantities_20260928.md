# V12 versus V13: animal production, collection and sales

Across the eight completed development worlds, V13 sells 78 more animal-product units: **32 more units actually produced, 33 fewer held units lost at animal exits, and 13 fewer collected units discarded from private stock**. The mix matters: milk sales fall 21, wool rise 55 and eggs rise 44. This is a physical accounting identity across natural-world runs, not a same-shop profit estimate.

Every V12/V13 own-farm animal production event has zero capacity loss. Extra optional harvests therefore do not increase output by freeing a full animal's yield capacity in this panel. They collect existing output sooner; later service, retirement and shared-market changes alter subsequent production. The different natural shop sequences remain a confound for final profit attribution.

## What is proved

The audit reconstructs positions from saved actions and dawn states, checks 1,920 saved early own-position snapshots and all 2,880 daily animal-product collection ledgers across 16 games and both farms, then checks every surviving animal tile against the official refresh equation. It runs no agent or engine.

For an unchanged species/birth/tile cohort, retained biological output equals next-dawn held units minus the old held units left after actual collection. The official engine checks the second unfed night and removes escaping animals **before** production. A surviving scheduled production yields one plus the prior care bank if fed; its held cap then applies, and today's fed-and-cared bonus is banked afterward. The audit follows that order and does not count a hypothetical output on an escaping animal as actual production.

All own animal-product balances close through the final executed D29 H22 action. There is no final D29 night refresh. Final held animal output and final private animal-product stock are zero in these runs; observed stock losses are therefore separable from unsold leftovers. Rival private stock is absent; their zero full-season collection-minus-sales residual proves zero final stock and discard where applicable. Birth counts by species **and birth day** match between V12/V13 in all eight worlds, as do first-output event counts. No birth delay explains these differences.

## Why milk falls in cases 00 and 01

**00:** The nine cow births and first outputs are identical. V13 intentionally retires cow36, born D6, with first-unfed D18 and disappearance at D20 dawn; V12 retains it. Its output falls 24→12, while the other cows collectively gain seven, yielding the net five-unit loss. Total production events fall 77→72 while paid care bonus remains 168. All milk is collected and sold, with no held exit loss or stock discard. Milk revenue falls $1,882: five fewer units and a lower realized average price ($164.39→$159.97). Natural shops first diverge at D15. There is no evidence that these milk units were merely stranded awaiting harvest.

**01:** The same five cow births yield the same four first-output events; cow50 born D12 is intentionally retired before first output in both arms. V13's milk decline is **two fewer production events plus eleven fewer paid bonus units**, 110→97. Cow24 retires at D26 dawn instead of D29 and loses five units total; cow35 loses four bonus units, cow44 two, and cow45 two. Cow35's D22 bank is zero instead of two; at D28 cows35/45 are unfed and pay no care bonus. Some of those late feed reductions coincide with admitted retirement; a missing exact retirement-date match is not by itself proof of an execution fault.

Again, no milk is lost in tile capacity, at exits, or from private stock. The loss is mostly late: D24–29 sales fall by 20 and revenue by $3,008, offset partly by earlier sales. Season milk revenue falls $2,994 and average price $85.59→$66.20. The rival sells the same 108 milk in both arms but earns $2,378 less, illustrating the changed price context. Shops diverge at D21 (Ice Cream→Bakery) and D24 (Pet Cafe→Farmers Market). The lower milk price and reduced late service accompany those observations; these traces do not isolate which downstream decision caused which part of the final loss.

## Why case 06 regresses despite more wool

Own wool sales rise 204→232: **15 more biological units, ten fewer held units lost at intended exits, and three fewer stock discards**. All 15 extra biological units are paid care bonus; actual production-event count stays 59. Own wool revenue rises $10,587. The rival also responds: biological wool and sales rise 240→276, comprising two extra production events and 34 extra bonus units; its wool revenue rises $12,714. Both farms' average wool prices rise, $141.67→$170.21 for ours and $146.18→$173.17 for the rival.

The first six revealed shops match. At D21 dawn, before the differing new shop trades, own cash is already +$469 and rival cash −$645: a +$1,114 margin difference. D21 then reveals Yarn Store in V13 versus Bakery in V12. Across the full natural games own cash gains $8,674 but rival cash gains $9,723, giving the **−$1,049 margin regression**. This cannot be described as a failure to execute the extra wool harvests or as proof that their same-shop profit is negative.

## Why cases 03 and 07 improve

**03 gains $2,619 margin despite selling four fewer milk and five fewer wool, with one more egg.** Own cash gains $4,485 and rival cash $1,866. The main own revenue gains are carrots (+$4,754; sales 107→186) and strawberries (+$3,284; 205→207); wheat revenue falls $2,051 and animal revenue falls $2,037. D18 changes Bakery→Farmers Market and D21 Ice Cream→Pet Cafe. At D18 dawn before the first changed shop trades, margin is −$30. The final improvement is therefore not evidence of a large direct animal-harvest quantity gain.

**07 gains $2,774 margin**, own cash +$10,386 and rival +$7,612. Own milk gains eleven biological/collected units, but three are discarded from private stock, leaving eight more sold. Wool gains four biological units plus three fewer held exit losses, yielding seven more sold. Milk/wool revenues rise $4,555/$7,611. The rival's milk quantity stays 174 while revenue rises $3,411; wool sales rise 196→212 and revenue $7,061. At the first differing-shop dawn D21 the margin improvement is already $3,728; the final difference is smaller. Shops change D21 Brunch→Smoothie and D24 Brunch→Yarn, so final price gains are not isolated collection timing gains.

## Actual optional exchanges and remaining limits

The separate full-eight execution audit finds 67 accepted target harvests collecting 214 already-held units (62 milk, 120 wool, 32 eggs). All target tiles/species/quantities match successful daily collection. The sacrificed commands are 27 WATER, 18 FERTILIZE, 13 COLLECT_FERTILIZER and nine CARE; none is later executed elsewhere at its recorded removed tile. These 214 units must **not** be reported as incremental production or even incremental season collection: the aggregate season collection gain is 65 animal-product units.

Sixty of 67 planned worker identities match the actual harvesting unit. Seven are remapped in actual execution: 03 D14/D25, 04 D16/D26, 05 D18, 06 D18 and 07 D21. The audit preserves these discrepancies rather than silently treating the planner log as an executed route. All accepted target-product collection-day stock balances have zero discard. The five modeled-over-100 days separately close across all shed items in `docs/kb115lt2_v13_capacity_audit_20260928.md`; V13's three-unit season milk discard is on another day in case07.

This evidence supports two separate mechanisms: earlier collection reduces some exit/stock losses, while later service and economic decisions change actual biological output. It does not establish an optimum feeding/retirement policy or justify feeding every retiring animal. Same-shop responsive tests remain necessary for causal full-season margin attribution.

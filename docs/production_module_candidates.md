# Recorded production modules worth an executor proof

Snapshot used: the 30 field-matched replays of Majkel submission 56216119 and Mother-Goose submission 56266758 in `results/fresh/leader_segments/`, cross-checked against the decoded segment ledgers and the published shop sequence in each record. These are executable hypotheses, not a discovered policy or a profitability result.

## Strongest concrete module: demand-gated tomato cohort, planted days 12-14

**Trigger from information actually visible at planting:** at the day-12 boundary (four shops unlocked), consider a small tomato cohort only when Pizza Shop or Farmers Market is already unlocked and current projected tomato supply leaves headroom against the visible shop sink. Those shops each demand tomatoes at 6 units/day per instance. Do not count any future shop draw. The conditional planting evidence is real: across the leaders, a newly revealed tomato-demand shop predicts +0.85/+1.64 tomato plantings over the following two segments (Majkel/Mother-Goose; within-segment permutation p=.018/.0007). This is evidence of a demand response, not proof that this exact trigger is their rule.

**Schedule and commitment:** tomato planted day 12, 13, or 14; seed cost 50. The new plant starts at yield 0. Water on planting day and at least every other day thereafter to avoid crop loss; reserve care through its four consecutive productions at ages 8, 9, 10, 11 (production resolves at midnight, so planting day 12 produces at the end of day 19 and can be harvested from day 20). Harvest each output promptly; unit inventory goes to the shed at day end and market SELL orders draw from the shed. Don't rely on a midnight tile harvest or keep the plot occupied after its fourth production. Budget one tile and explicit future worker visits through the last harvest. Fertilizer is optional and should be tested as a separate arm: the applying hand needs fertilizer in its own inventory; fertilize and water by the end of a production day to double that output.

**Recorded scale:** Mother-Goose plants a mean 4.9 tomatoes in days 12-14 (range 0-15) and 9.5 total per game (0-25); tomato revenue is 7,647 mean over the sample. The own V45 upload plants 1.3 (0 or 10) and earns 2,937 in its different, near-clone match pool. The price/revenue comparison is pool-confounded; the within-game shop response and presence of these cohorts are the stronger evidence. Tomato has only 4 units/tile total at base, so this is a small local module rather than a broad land conversion.

**Replacement candidate:** replace a wheat planting visit on day 12-14 only if the executor can own the tile and guarantee the care/harvest loop. Do not replace an already-planted strawberry: that destroys its remaining scheduled production. Existing cohort experiments show that inserting a crop without reserving future labor is unsafe; their tested tomato-return-time swaps lost margin, so the module needs a real tile-owner and labor reservation, not a seed-name substitution.

## Secondary module: late strawberry replenishment, days 12-16

When Brunch Spot, Ice Cream Shop, Smoothie Shop, or Farmers Market is visible, and projected strawberry inventory plus standing yields leave room under current absorption, plant a small cohort on days 12-16. These shops demand strawberries at 6/day per instance; use only currently unlocked shops in the trigger. One plant costs 100, starts at yield 0, and gives four total units at ages 10, 12, 14, 16. For planting day 12, production resolves at the end of days 21, 23, 25, 27; units can be harvested on days 22, 24, 26, 28 and sold from the shed after deposit. Planting day 16 yields harvested on days 26, 28, 30, 32, so only the first two fit before season end (day 29). Water at planting, then often enough to avoid two consecutive missed days; reserve harvest visits on each in-season yield. Fertilizer requires the applying hand to carry it and water on the production day; test it separately.

Mother-Goose averages 10.6 day-12+ strawberry plantings (vs Majkel 4.5 and our 0), and the shop event study gives +3.31 plants after a newly revealed strawberry shop for either leader (p=.0002 each). This identifies a reliable association with shop arrivals, not that day-12+ strawberries are always profitable. The imitation experiment that cut our strawberry supply without a coordinated replacement lost badly; a demand-gated addition/replacement must account for standing strawberry cohorts and market supply, not clone a count blindly.

## Short-cycle alternative: carrot cohort, days 18-23

Consider one native planting slot only when Pet Cafe (2x carrot demand) or Farmers Market is visible and carrot supply is demonstrably short. Carrot seed costs 20 and starts at yield 1; water on planting day or it weeds at day end. Water on ages 2 and 3 for three units unfertilized; fertilizing by age 1 and watering on ages 2 and 3 can reach the four-unit cap. Harvest by age 3, after the age-3 water. This is a much shorter delivery horizon than tomato or strawberry and can fit late reveals, but leader association is weaker as a reusable fixed module: carrot plantings correlate with demand across games and rise +3.31 after a new carrot-demand shop for Mother-Goose (p=.008), while its mean day-18 carrot count is only 1.1 (range 0-12); Majkel's is 3.9 (0-21). Don't transplant the later 24-26 carrot ramp as a rule: its horizon and planted counts are entangled with each tape's state.

## Decision and limits

Start executor proof with the **day-12 tomato cohort** because it adds a distinct product with a clear, observable demand gate and four concentrated harvest days. Keep the strawberry cohort as a second arm only behind a supply headroom check; the leaders' evidence for it is stronger in quantity but it is the crop most vulnerable to shared-market glut. Test each cohort against a no-op insertion on held-out natural shop sequences, both seats, and fixed opponents; report own cash as well as margin. The 52 recent matches establish what was planted and when, and the event study controls seasonal segment timing. They do not reveal whether the leaders read shop names or price signals, their exact thresholds, or causal value of any particular cohort. For Majkel, identical observations sometimes precede different actions, so no exact decision rule can be recovered. No future shops or opponent outcomes are used in these proposed triggers.

# Unused own-suffix accounting helper

[semantic_strategy_own_suffix_20260928.py](../scripts/semantic_strategy_own_suffix_20260928.py) projects an already-admitted, anonymous own-farm suffix. Nothing in the active policy imports it. Existing policy/block files and frozen V14 are unchanged. Fourteen [pure accounting tests](../tests/test_semantic_own_suffix_20260928.py) pass; no engine, training export, full192-state replay or timed benchmark ran.

The proposed API is `own_suffix_calendar(state, forecast, config, crop_rules, animal_rules, output_calendar)`. Injecting the existing output function makes the biological approximation explicit. The result includes daily gross output, internal wheat/fertilizer use, initial-stock credits, signed net-flow proxies, crop releases, occupied animal/retirement counts, structures, cohort calendars and warnings. It reads only own state and the supplied modeled count schedule. Rival state, rival private stock, future observations, episode identity and future shop fields are ignored.

## Current-day convention and invariants

The first forecast row must be today; subsequent dates must be unique and increasing. Gaps mean no new commitments. Initial cohorts already exist at today's dawn. Modeled animal/ongoing output is dated to dawn; annual output remains on the supplied model's harvest day. Today's releases and establishment jobs then determine end-of-day occupancy. New animals require feed on their placement day. Crops planted today first produce at the model's later date.

Each initial or newly planned cohort receives exactly one output calendar and one crop fertilizer calendar. Those dated contributions are summed once, rather than regenerating and adding the same lifetime on every future day. Partial initial annual readiness splits release counts between today and tomorrow. New plantings receive no predecessor yield, watering or active-fertilizer metadata. Initial duplicate species/birth crop rows are rejected until aggregated, avoiding ambiguous reuse of observed detail counts.

Retirement counts select oldest active anonymous cohorts, matching the legacy suffix convention. They do not infer intent from hunger. Existing cohort-specific intent is retained; an aggregate committed count lacking its exact delay conservatively starts today. A retirement on D remains physically occupied on D and D+1, exits at dawn D+2, and receives no modeled feed after intent starts. Structures remain until an explicit removal. Attempting to retire more active animals than exist fails; occupied-animal/pen and land-capacity inconsistencies are returned as warnings for a future caller to reject or inspect.

Own initial shed/carried WHEAT and FERTILIZER offset internal use once, chronologically. For each day/product the identity is `signed_net = gross_output - internal_use + initial_stock_credit`; unused stock remains explicit. Other products equal gross modeled output. This is not actual delivery/sales accounting, and no rival private credit is invented.

## Inherited assumptions and an explicit discrepancy

The helper preserves output_calendar's care level, production dates, retirement-period fertilizer output, end-season clamping and omission of D29 feed. Gross current-dawn animal/ongoing output and existing held field inventory retain the legacy approximation; they are not actual newly received, collected or sold goods. It does not repair overdue held crop output, cap loss, worker feasibility, delivery delay, market sales at price1 or gross suffix cash. Past-due observed crops and fertilizer appearing after retirement intent are explicitly flagged. Ongoing last modeled production is retained before its configured release; no extra harvest is invented at release.

There is one important difference from repeatedly invoking the legacy suffix model. Suppose two wheat crops reach their release day with one ready and one requiring another day. The origin model explicitly schedules5 units today and6 tomorrow. `_advance` retains the unready crop's original birth, after which a new `output_calendar` invocation tomorrow can omit its6 units because the nominal release is yesterday. The helper preserves the origin-dated6 once and records `origin_dated_receipt_differs_from_legacy_next_day_recomputation` with both quantities. The regression also plants a replacement today and proves it produces its own later5 units without inheriting fertilizer credit. This is an integration behavior change to disclose, not a claim that the active policy already preserves the receipt.

## Integration design, not authorization

First use the helper diagnostically on the existing first-pass admitted suffix. The forecast currently extrapolates animal fertilizer through the season but stops crop input when current crops finish; the already-generated suffix contains the missing planned replacement births and retirement intentions. Its own calendar can quantify that asymmetry without fitting to development outcomes. Before runtime integration, current and future physical-occupancy warnings require an explicit rejection/fallback policy. Merely logging those warnings and continuing to count the infeasible suffix as supply is not acceptable; this unused helper currently reports them for inspection.

A later optional integration should be bounded: form the ordinary first-pass suffix, capture its anonymous calendar, perform at most one current-day revaluation, and reconstruct the suffix once. Do not iterate to convergence or consume observed future cohorts. Current commitment memory remains based on real establishments/intent, never on simulated success. Replan from actual observations tomorrow. Preserve a fast fallback if modeling exceeds its budget.

**Do not feed this complete suffix into the existing `cohort_value` unchanged.** The full suffix includes today's selected additions; scoring those additions again as extra would count their supply twice, and the old competitive term describes only current cohorts. Define one baseline consistently. The safer accounting contract is a full baseline plan plus a bounded one-unit removal/replacement alternative, comparing their signed calendars and capital costs; reject an alternative whose fixed future retirement counts or capacity become inconsistent. A cheaper baseline that omits today's unbought additions needs an explicit reconciliation rule for later dependent births/retirements. That choice remains unresolved and is required before runtime integration.

The same marginal comparison must include negative wheat/fertilizer inputs in market-price effects and preserve persistent inventory differences across future days. Charging fertilizer only at baseline price, as V14 does, remains an approximation. Cash, seed/animal purchases, wages and land are not calculated here; the existing `_advance` cash estimate is unchanged and must not be described as newly corrected.

Rival future rotations are a separate model, not the own suffix copied onto another farm. Existing modern100 rows can support causal public-cohort/shop birth and exit-count predictions, but verified enriched banks, service and market trajectories are still unavailable until the prepared source replay runs. Any older rival-library reuse needs a fresh reserved-ID audit. No rival fitting, scenarios or additional source replay is part of this helper.

Before any gameplay: independently review these conventions; add baseline/alternative marginal and no-op identity checks; replay saved observations without outcome-based tuning; report current versus forecast-only decisions and runtime separately. Only a separately frozen optional integration should reach a paired experiment. The helper and tests alone authorize no game.
